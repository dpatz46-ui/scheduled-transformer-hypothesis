# The Scheduled Transformer

## A Hypothesis of Depth-Indexed State Regimes in Pre-RMSNorm Language Models

**Proposed by Dustin Patzer, Independent Researcher**

### Research statement

I propose the **Scheduled Transformer hypothesis**: modern pre-normalized transformer language models do not merely apply a succession of nominally similar residual transformations. Rather, computation proceeds through a reproducible, ordered sequence of **depth-indexed state regimes**.

In its narrowest and presently most operational form, the hypothesis concerns the geometry of the residual stream across depth. Let \(x_{i,p,l}\) denote the residual state for example \(i\), token position \(p\), and depth \(l\). Define its radial coordinate as

\[
r_{i,p,l}=\|x_{i,p,l}\|,
\]

and, where useful,

\[
y_{i,p,l}=\log r_{i,p,l}.
\]

The central claim is not simply that residual norms tend to grow with layer depth. Depth-dependent residual norm growth has already been observed in prior work. The proposed object is instead the **ordered sequence of layer-conditioned state distributions and transition regimes itself**: their centres, widths, shapes, overlaps, persistence, reorganization, contraction and expansion, and reproducible departures associated with particular contexts or computations.

A **depth schedule** is therefore a reproducible sequence

\[
\mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{R}_0,\mathcal{R}_1,\dots,\mathcal{R}_L),
\]

where each \(\mathcal{R}_l\) represents a layer-indexed state regime or distributional band, and where the transition from one regime to the next depends upon the inherited state produced by preceding depth.

The proposal is consequently stronger than the observation that later residual states are larger than earlier ones. It predicts that transformer depth is **non-exchangeable at the level of state dynamics**: different depths occupy systematically different regimes and perform transformations conditioned upon states that have themselves been produced by the preceding sequence.

The residual recurrence can be written schematically as

\[
x_{l+1}=x_l+\Delta_l,
\]

or, resolving the principal branches of a standard pre-normalized decoder block,

\[
x_l^{mid}=x_l^{pre}+\Delta_l^A,
\]

\[
x_l^{out}=x_l^{mid}+\Delta_l^M.
\]

The Scheduled Transformer hypothesis concerns the structured trajectory generated by this recursive process. Each layer receives not an arbitrary input but an inherited state shaped by the transformations preceding it. The resulting model is therefore better described, on this hypothesis, as traversing a learned or emergent depth-indexed state schedule than as merely repeating an otherwise homogeneous computational operation.

## Preliminary evidence

Preliminary experimental work strongly indicates support for this hypothesis.

Across several model families and previously collected activation datasets, residual-state trajectories show reproducible depth structure that appears richer than simple monotonic norm growth. Observed phenomena include depth-specific radial bands, substantial changes in distributional width, repeated phases of radial contraction and expansion, persistence and reorganization of relative state positions across depth, and candidate context-conditioned departures from the dominant trajectory.

One useful diagnostic follows from the log-radius transition

\[
y_{l+1}=y_l+d_l,
\]

for which

\[
\operatorname{Var}(y_{l+1})
=
\operatorname{Var}(y_l)
+
\operatorname{Var}(d_l)
+
2\operatorname{Cov}(y_l,d_l).
\]

Where the radial distribution contracts,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(y_{l+1})<\operatorname{Var}(y_l),
\]

it follows necessarily that

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(y_l,d_l)<-\frac12\operatorname{Var}(d_l).
\]

Such transitions cannot be described merely as independent accumulation of residual magnitude. They imply that the radial increment is systematically related to inherited radial state: states lying higher in the distribution receive, on average, smaller or more-negative radial increments than states lying lower in it.

Initial primary-data analyses contain repeated depth-specific examples of this behavior.

These observations are presently treated as **supporting preliminary evidence rather than completed confirmation**. Some components, especially the dominant depth dependence of residual magnitude, overlap with and extend observations already reported in prior residual-stream research. The distinctive proposal here is to elevate the **sequence of layer-conditioned regimes and transitions** to the primary object of analysis.

## Context-conditioned sub-schedules

The hypothesis further permits, but does not require for every computation, the existence of **context-conditioned sub-schedules**.

A sub-schedule is not simply a prompt whose radial trajectory has a slightly different overall slope. It is a reproducible departure from the dominant depth schedule over a contiguous depth interval, potentially followed by reintegration with the dominant trajectory.

One operational form is to remove the dominant schedule together with prompt-specific offset and linear slope,

\[
e_{i,l}=y_{i,l}-\mu_l-\alpha_i-\beta_i l,
\]

and test for structured residual excursions that depart, persist, and later reconverge.

Preliminary observations include candidate examples of this form, including unusually localized trajectories associated with particular arithmetic computations. These remain targets for deliberate replication rather than established general results.

## Formalization and confirmatory testing

A systematic empirical formalization of the hypothesis is being pursued.

The planned program uses full residual-state extraction from modern pre-RMSNorm models rather than relying only on aggregate norm measurements. For each transformer block, the intended measurements include

\[
x_l^{pre},\quad \Delta_l^A,\quad x_l^{mid},\quad \Delta_l^M,\quad x_l^{out},
\]

together with normalization-state information where practical.

The extraction methodology has already been technically validated on the official base checkpoint of Llama 3.2 3B. Hook-based reconstruction verifies the intended residual boundaries to numerical precision appropriate to FP16 execution, including exact continuity between adjacent captured block boundaries.

The confirmatory regime will preserve full token-level trajectories, including prompt and generated tokens, and will test hundreds of frozen prompts spanning ordinary language, matched semantic conditions, coherence controls, relational tasks, arithmetic, multi-step computation, and targeted replications of earlier observations.

The objective is not merely to find another example of residual-norm growth. The hypothesis will instead be tested against stronger predictions: reproducible depth-specific distributions; systematic changes in spread and shape; non-independent transitions between inherited radial position and subsequent radial increment; repeated reorganization boundaries; and, where present, reproducible context-conditioned excursions from and reintegration with the dominant schedule.

## Scope of the claim

The Scheduled Transformer hypothesis does **not** propose that residual radius alone represents meaning, nor that a transformer explicitly reads residual magnitude as a numerical layer index. Direction, subspace structure, and other geometric properties of the residual state remain available to carry differentiated relational content.

The narrower proposal is that residual states occupy structured, depth-dependent regimes and that transformer computation proceeds through the ordered traversal of those regimes.

> **Compressed statement:** The Scheduled Transformer hypothesis proposes that transformer computation proceeds through a reproducible sequence of depth-indexed state regimes, with each successive transformation acting upon an inherited state shaped by the preceding trajectory. Preliminary primary-data work strongly supports the existence of such scheduling in the residual stream; systematic formalization and confirmatory testing are underway.
