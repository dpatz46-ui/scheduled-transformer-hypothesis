# Research roadmap

## Stage 1 — public statement

Publish the narrow Scheduled Transformer hypothesis, its scope, preliminary empirical status, and the pre-specified confirmatory regime.

Goal: establish a public, citable statement of the proposed object without waiting for completion of the full empirical paper.

## Stage 2 — confirmatory radial study

Complete the frozen prompt harvest on the primary base model and analyze:

- layer-conditioned radial distributions;
- distributional width and shape;
- contraction/expansion transitions;
- inherited-state / increment covariance;
- persistence and reorganization;
- targeted sub-schedule candidates.

This stage should remain the narrow first empirical paper.

## Stage 3 — replication

Replicate the dominant result across additional dense pre-RMSNorm model families and relevant historical/post-LN contrasts.

## Stage 4 — functional role

Test whether schedule position affects downstream transformability, relative-write authority, and intervention outcomes.

Candidate quantities include

\[
\rho_l=\frac{\|\Delta_l\|}{\|x_l\|}
\]

and parallel/perpendicular write decomposition.

## Stage 5 — mechanism

Investigate normalization geometry, sparse radial contributions, branch-specific writes, gamma effects, and possible state/subspace locking.

## Stage 6 — broader consequences

Only after the core phenomenon is established should the program test broader hypotheses involving circuit mediation, catastrophic forgetting/interference, training dynamics, architecture scaling, or a wider theory of intelligent computation.
